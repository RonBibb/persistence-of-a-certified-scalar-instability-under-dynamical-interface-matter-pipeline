# Discussion and conclusion draft

## Discussion

The paired theorem resolves a specific ambiguity in the source-pinned static
model. The positive-curvature branch is not absent: it exists, is locally
unique on the certified first-event interval, and is reproduced by an
independent interval-ODE implementation. What fails is the simultaneous demand
that the same fixed action and wall potentials realize that branch at the
source-prescribed proper separation `D0`. Over the complete declared launch
range, the fixed-distance wall residual cannot vanish on the
positive-curvature route.

This conclusion is stronger than a failed shooting calculation but narrower
than a generic nonexistence theorem. The 693-box D2A cover rules out a missed
launch value within exact `0<=a<=v0`, and the residual-vector infinity-norm
lower bound shows that the exclusion is not a tolerance-scale near miss. It
does not rule out a solution outside that launch interval, at zero or negative
curvature, for different wall potentials, or outside the maximally symmetric
warped ansatz.
Nor does local uniqueness of the D1 root imply global uniqueness across all
possible first-event branches.

The factor-of-two separation contrast gives the result a direct geometric
interpretation. If the upper wall is allowed to occur where its first Israel
condition is met, the remaining scalar condition selects a regular curved
solution near `D=1.9975`. If the wall is instead imposed at
`D0=0.9996389747145851`, the two upper conditions are incompatible throughout
the certified positive-curvature launch class. The model therefore selects a
different static geometry than the one encoded by the original proper
interval. This is a compatibility statement among field equations, wall data,
curvature, and separation. It is not evidence of a force that dynamically
moves a wall from `D0` to D1.

The distinction is especially important because the parameter point has a
pre-existing source ancestry. Holding the exact literals fixed prevents the
successful D1 branch from being manufactured by retuning the model after the
fixed-distance failure. At the same time, source ancestry does not supply a
dynamical bridge. The static D1 solution has a different proper separation and
positive four-curvature; in a flat de Sitter slicing its Cauchy data also carry
nonzero extrinsic curvature. A collapse development cannot be asserted to
select this branch without an independently constrained embedding and
evolution.

The result fits naturally into the established scalar-brane picture while
adding a different standard of inference. Earlier work shows that scalar and
wall potentials can determine both separation and induced curvature, casts
junction conditions as curves in a low-dimensional phase space, finds zero,
one, or several static configurations by shooting, and studies instability
between configurations. The present result does not replace or generalize that
physics. Its contribution is to certify, for one exact model, both sides of a
compatibility statement: where the desired branch exists and where it cannot
exist over a complete declared launch class.

The two computer-assisted components play different roles. D1 uses validated
flow, event transversality, sensitivity propagation, and interval Newton to
prove existence and local uniqueness. The independent Taylor--Picard replay
substantially reduces the risk of a producer-specific existence artifact. D2A
uses an exact contiguous cover and terminal exclusion rules to prove absence at
fixed separation. D2A has not been regenerated by a second independent
interval-ODE producer; its authority rests on the frozen Arb implementation,
source audit, exact guards, stored terminal cover, and post-run replay. This is
a software-assurance limitation worth disclosing, but it does not change what
the current certificate proves if those verified enclosures are accepted.

The most informative next static extension would not be another root search at
the same point. A prospectively fixed parameter neighborhood could test
whether the mismatch persists under perturbations of the inherited literals,
and a spectral calculation could address stability of the D1 branch. Either
would be a new theorem with its own domain and gates. For the dynamical line,
the first necessary step is more basic: construct and admit Cauchy data that
use the same action and boundary equations. Only after a short-development
matrix closes the bulk, wall, corner, domain, and representation controls would
MOTS tracking or backward-null event-horizon estimation have a defensible
same-lineage basis.

## Conclusion

We have established a paired computer-assisted result for a fixed
five-dimensional Einstein--scalar two-wall boundary-value problem. A locally
unique positive-curvature solution exists when the upper-wall separation is
selected by its first transverse Israel event, with certified proper separation
near `1.9975`. An independent validated producer reproduces the root and
encloses its essential geometry. For the same action, wall parameters,
orientations, and launch family, an exhaustive exact cover excludes every
positive-curvature simultaneous wall root at the prescribed separation
`D0=0.9996389747145851` over exact `0<=a<=v0`. The selected separation is
rigorously between `1.998218966` and `1.998341081` times `D0`.

The scientific conclusion is therefore precise: the originally prescribed
distance is incompatible with the certified curved static branch within the
declared reduced family. The result converts an ambiguous numerical failure
into an existence-and-exclusion theorem. It does not establish stability,
dynamical selection, collapse, a trapped region, an event horizon, or a baby
universe. Those remain separate questions rather than interpretations of the
static certificate.
