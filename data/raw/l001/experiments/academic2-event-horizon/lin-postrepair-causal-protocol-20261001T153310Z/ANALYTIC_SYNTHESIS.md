# Analytic synthesis — existence and localization are separate problems

## Main result of this window

The causal program does not have to wait for a terminal-MOTS null family to
converge before it can address horizon existence.

In one physically qualified development, suppose:

1. a future weakly trapped cap `C` satisfies the hypotheses of the finite
   barrier theorem relative to a genuine physical exterior `E`, so every event
   of `C` lies outside `J^-(E)`;
2. an exterior worldtube portion `W` is proved to lie in `E`; and
3. one event `p_v` on the same connected slice has a continuum causal curve to
   `W`.

Choose any \(p_c\in C\) and a continuous spatial launch curve from \(p_c\) to
\(p_v\).  The causal bracket lemma forces that curve to meet
\(H_E=\partial J^-(E)\).  This establishes an end-relative horizon intersection
without treating a finite computational wall as infinity and without assuming
that a late MOTS is already close to the event horizon.

The backward-null calculation has a different job: efficiently locate that
intersection and test whether late terminal choices produce the same locator.
If it fails to settle while the causal bracket succeeds, the failure belongs
to the estimator, not to horizon existence.

### One barrier cylinder can organize all three roles

The most economical construction uses one all-future physical cylinder
\(\mathcal T_R\) and the immediately outer physical end
\(E_R=\{r>R\}\):

1. strict outward expansion on every section, together with the other
   finite-barrier hypotheses, prevents the trapped cap from reaching the
   cylinder; separation then prevents it from reaching \(E_R\);
2. a continuum curve through the cylinder and into a certified outer portion
   \(W\subset E_R\) gives a positive visibility witness; and
3. the captured cap event and an event that reaches \(W\) through the cylinder
   provide the two ends of a causal bracket.

If the intended exterior \(E\) begins at another radius \(R_0<R\), the
slow-time exterior argument must prove
\(J^-(E_R)=J^-(E)\) before the two relative horizons are identified.  A theorem
that explicitly includes the cylinder in the end can instead use arrival on
the cylinder itself.  What cannot be omitted is same-development exterior
connectivity, barrier separation and all-future expansion positivity.

## Why this matters for the current blockage

O2's coherent terminal-time offset no longer creates a serial blockade.  It
showed that P407's terminal MOTSs were not suitable null-sheet surrogates over
the tested epoch.  O4 then blocked physical causal use of that lineage unless
the compatibility problem is repaired.  A new companion can therefore pursue
two questions in parallel:

- **Causal existence/bracketing:** exterior plus independently certified
  opposite fates.
- **Geometric localization:** terminal-MOTS null fronts, their first-variation
  injection `b`, their transport rate `lambda`, and their numerical error
  envelope.

Only their later agreement identifies the terminal-front estimator with the
relative horizon.

## The remaining hard hypotheses

This separation does not make the global problem easy.  The captured endpoint
still requires exact or rigorously enclosed cap expansions, physical NCC,
admissible brane contacts, causal compactness and an all-future positive
separating cylinder (or another capture theorem).  The visible endpoint needs
\(W\subset E\) and a continuum causal-curve enclosure.  A short numerical
companion can test margins and reject bad constructions; by itself it cannot
prove the all-future statements.

The most valuable analytic target is consequently an exterior continuation or
matching result that makes one cylinder physical and preserves its positive
outward expansion.  The most valuable bounded numerical target is a short
bulk-wall-compatible companion that determines whether the MOTS, cylinder
margins and candidate opposite witnesses survive the repair.

## Constructive failure meanings

- **No trapped-cap authority:** capture is unresolved; do not infer it from a
  ray that later moves inward.
- **No exterior authority:** arrivals at the numerical wall remain trajectory
  diagnostics, not visibility.
- **Opposite witnesses but no terminal localization:** a relative horizon is
  bracketed; replace or defer the MOTS-terminal estimator.
- **Terminal localization but no opposite witnesses:** a stable null surface
  is measured; its global causal identity is unresolved.
- **Both, but no overlap within errors:** the estimator and the causal bracket
  identify different objects or one error budget is incomplete.  Stop rather
  than average them together.
