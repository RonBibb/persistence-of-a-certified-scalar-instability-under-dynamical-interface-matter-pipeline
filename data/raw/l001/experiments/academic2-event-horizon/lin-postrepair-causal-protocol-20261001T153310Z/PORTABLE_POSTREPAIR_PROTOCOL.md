# Portable post-repair causal experiment

## Scientific question

After a new bulk-wall-compatible companion has been admitted, can one
distinguish three logically different statements:

1. late MOTS-seeded null fronts become terminal-independent in the represented
   geometry;
2. events on an earlier launch curve have independently certified opposite
   fates relative to one physical exterior; and
3. the terminal-independent front lies inside a shrinking causal-fate bracket?

Only the combination of the second and third statements can support a located
end-relative event-horizon result.  The first statement alone remains a null
surface estimator.

## Why this is the correct successor to O1/O2/O4

O1 exactly reproduced terminal surfaces and O2 showed that their null fronts
retained a coherent terminal-time offset.  O4 then found that the underlying
P407 evolution is not physically qualified for an event-horizon inference.
Therefore the P407 numerical values are controls of the represented geometry,
not reusable physical terminal data.  This protocol ports the *experiment*,
not those states or their outcome thresholds, to a new admitted companion.

## Gate 0 — companion admissibility and promotion

### Gate 0A — pilot-admissible companion

No causal pilot starts until one immutable companion authority binds:

- the same action, bulk equations, wall equations, gauge, time orientation and
  physical interval used by the causal code;
- simultaneous bulk and wall residual gates, including the wall limit of the
  physical equations rather than only the implemented boundary rows;
- source, runtime, initial state, restart, spacetime-interpolation and output
  hashes; and
- a declared numerical-error inventory.  Solver tolerance and evolution
  resolution are separate entries; neither is a continuum proof.

Gate 0A permits candidate terminal traces, candidate worldtube measurements
and code portability checks.  Every outcome remains explicitly numerical.

### Gate 0B — claim-grade promotion

Before any supported physical causal outcome, require at least two bulk
evolution resolutions, and at least two independently solved outer-domain
locations whenever the exterior/worldtube is used, together with a declared
physical-error budget covering both transfers.

Failure of Gate 0A stops the physical causal line.  Passing 0A but not 0B
permits inexpensive pilots without upgrading their meaning.  Neither outcome
reverses the finite-resolution MOTS/dynamical-horizon result.

## Lane A — terminal-sheet estimator

### A1. Portable O1 terminal interface

Track one outer-MOTS lineage on at least four prospectively selected terminal
times `T0<T1<T2<T3` from the same admitted evolution.  At every terminal retain
the surface profile, outgoing stability mode, both expansions, contact data,
full leaf certificate, and exact predecessor/source bindings.  Use at least
three surface resolutions.  No failed terminal may be replaced after seeing
the result.

### A2. Portable O2 null family

Evolve the outgoing null front from each terminal backward over one common
spacetime interval.  Retain coordinate and Hamiltonian formulations, strict
integration tolerance, angular-label refinement, surface-resolution transfer,
spacetime-interpolation control, evolution-resolution transfer and domain
transfer.  Every front must pass domain, null-defect, projection/coverage,
caustic, crossover, ordering and physical-boundary-contact gates.

At every common observation slice compute a symmetric proper Hausdorff
diameter `D_T` of the terminal family in a declared physical spatial metric.
Build a numerical enclosure `E_num` from the maximum independently measured
method/resolution/domain control, not from the spread of the primary family.
Report both model-length units and local native-cell units.

The represented fronts are localized at a slice only when

`D_T + 2 E_num <= delta_loc`,

where `delta_loc` is frozen in physical units before production.  The number
of required slices is frozen with the observation schedule.  This is a
finite-archive result, not an all-terminal limit.

### A3. Terminal-tail diagnostic

For every terminal and generator compute or enclose

`b = h(N, M_T + beta) - alpha`

and the null-front normal rate

`lambda = N(alpha) - alpha K(N,N)`.

The first-variation injection bound is

`B_t(T)=sup_y |b(T,y)| exp(integral_T^t lambda ds)`.

Finite data may report a tail model only as descriptive.  A terminal-Cauchy
claim requires a justified integrable upper envelope for `B_t`, including the
rim and all generators, and a tail integral smaller than `delta_loc`.  A
positive rate at selected points or a small seed secant is insufficient.

Lane-A outcomes are:

- `REPRESENTED-TERMINAL-LOCALIZATION-SUPPORTED`;
- `VALID-NO-TERMINAL-LOCALIZATION`; or
- `TECHNICALLY-INCOMPLETE`.

None authorizes an event-horizon claim.

## Lane B — physical exterior and opposite causal witnesses

### Implementation boundary

Do not invent a second fate vocabulary.  Reuse the strict event/end/lineage
identity and reviewed-resolver boundary in
`launch-fate-interface-2026-09-12`.  Its 14 logic controls were replayed in
this window with zero failures.  A production successor still needs a
versioned native authority adapter: the older optional P408-R2 positive path
contains an undefined `_validate_native_artifacts` call, and a permissive stub
would erase rather than repair the trust boundary.

### B1. Declare the exterior

Define one same-development physical future exterior `E`.  Bind its topology,
time extent, brane/corner treatment and quantitative onset radius `R0`.
Choose one of two routes:

- **visibility-only route:** prove the exact sampled worldtube portion
  \(W\subset E\); or
- **complete-surrogate route:** prove the stronger, time-qualified equality
  `J^-(W)=J^-(E)`.

The computational outer boundary, a Sommerfeld condition, positive sampled
cylinder expansion, or monotone radial coordinate does not supply either
authority.

When capture uses a barrier at \(R>R_0\), distinguish the immediately outer
end \(E_R=\{r>R\}\) from the larger declared exterior \(E\).  The barrier
separates the cap from \(E_R\); equality of their causal pasts is required to
transfer that captured-set statement to \(E\).  The positive visibility target
may be a certified portion just outside the barrier.  Do not assume that a
cylinder lying inside \(E\) automatically separates the cap from all of \(E\).

### B2. Visible witnesses

On a connected launch curve at a fixed physical slice, enclose a continuum
future causal curve from at least one launch event to \(W\subset E\).  Bind event
identity independently of its proposed fate.  Trace residuals, interpolation,
metric error and worldtube-contact error must fit inside the enclosure.

### B3. Captured witnesses

Certify at least one event on the same launch curve as outside `J^-(E)`.  The
preferred route is direct membership in a captured set obtained from the
finite-barrier theorem: an exact weakly trapped cap, physical NCC, umbilic
walls, causal compactness, a separating physical cylinder and strictly
positive outward cylinder expansion for all future time.  A numerical MOTS
with an unenclosed expansion residual is only a capture candidate.  A single
ray entering the candidate captured set is not a launch-event certificate.

### B4. Bracket and bracket atlas

Apply `CAUSAL_BRACKET_LEMMA.md` to each opposite-witness pair.  Refine the
launch parameter prospectively, preserving nested brackets.  The bracket width
is a physical arc length and includes event-location and metric uncertainties.
Freeze a physical target `delta_bracket` before witness production.  A bracket
is numerically resolved only when its certified-set separation plus both
endpoint-location/error allowances is at most that target; do not stop merely
because a sign change was found.

One opposing-fate pair brackets one intersection of \(H_E\), not an entire
horizon cross-section.  A cross-section result additionally requires a
predeclared connected family of launch curves covering every surface label,
including both physical rims; an admitted bracket on every curve; consistent
orientation; noncrossing ordering; and continuity/coverage sufficient to join
the brackets into an enclosing atlas.  Missing labels remain gaps rather than
being filled by an unconstrained interpolation.

For the exact SO(3)-reduced two-brane model, the natural atlas is a family of
outward radial launch curves on one slice at fixed compact coordinate \(z\),
with \(z\) covering the full retained interval.  Bracket width is measured in
the slice's physical radial arc length, not raw coordinate \(r\).  The two
brane endpoints require their own one-sided contact enclosures.  This symmetry
reduction may not be reused if a successor breaks SO(3).

Lane-B outcomes are:

- `CERTIFIED-END-RELATIVE-HORIZON-INTERSECTION-BRACKET`;
- `CERTIFIED-END-RELATIVE-HORIZON-CROSS-SECTION-BRACKET-ATLAS`;
- `NUMERICAL-OPPOSITE-WITNESS-CANDIDATE`; or
- `NO-ADMITTED-CAUSAL-BRACKET`.

## Lane C — identify the estimator with the causal bracket

At common earlier slices compare the Lane-A null front with the Lane-B launch
brackets in the same physical metric.  Require:

1. on every predeclared launch curve, the interval enclosing the front's
   crossing is contained in the corresponding certified causal bracket; mere
   overlap is retained only as a candidate;
2. the sum of front, projection, launch-location and metric error budgets is
   smaller than the final bracket width;
3. every bracket meets the frozen `delta_bracket` target;
4. terminal, evolution-resolution, domain and witness refinements do not move
   the front outside the next tighter bracket; and
5. the result is stable under at least one later terminal quartet not used to
   choose `delta_loc`.

If Lane B is certified but Lane A fails, the relative horizon is bracketed but
the MOTS-terminal construction is not a useful locator.  If Lane A passes but
Lane B is absent, the null front is terminal-stable but has no event-horizon
meaning.  This 2-by-2 separation prevents another long evolution from being
mistaken for the missing global argument.

Even a complete bracket atlas establishes only an enclosed intersection set on
the declared slice.  Smoothness, null-generator completeness, topology away
from the atlas, global first formation and identification with a separately
defined null infinity remain distinct statements.

The axes may be run concurrently after Gate 0 because terminal tracing,
surface-resolution transfer, domain transfer and authority review read the
same sealed companion without modifying it.

Recommended scheduling uses one evolution producer only.  Once it seals a
terminal block, independent read-only workers may perform the two null
formulations, resolution transfers and witness-candidate tracing.  Exterior
theorem review and finite-barrier hypothesis review are non-evolution work and
can proceed concurrently.  No worker may infer physical admission from another
worker's incomplete checkpoint.

## Stop rules

Stop rather than extend when the companion fails bulk-wall compatibility, the
same-development exterior cannot be specified, witness authorities refer to
different ends or lineages, or the causal bracket contradicts the terminal
front outside the full uncertainty budget.  Do not rescue a failed result by
moving the wall, dropping a terminal, enlarging an error floor, or replacing a
capture statement about the launch event with a later ray-entry statement.
