## Work packages and gates

### A. Function-space and source audit — first, bounded desk calculation

Write the exact norm and common complex collar before estimating a radius. List each unknown and its harmonic/derivative weights: scalar, conformal metric, trace metric, graph displacement, oscillator and any lower-wall clock readout. Translate the manuscript's source maps, but replace every charge-specific rate and denominator by the neutral expression and prove its bound.

Audit each required source derivative separately. The existing response estimates explicitly charge for derivatives of the trace, mixed and radial sources; do not assume analytic regularity removes their cost. Moving-wall evaluation must remain inside the selected collar. Include exponential, reciprocal and graph-normalization factors with declared distance from their singular sets.

**Gate A:** an explicit table mapping every response input to a bounded nonlinear source operation, with no unbudgeted derivative or boundary displacement. A reviewer should examine this table before a numerical radius search.

### B. Common-collar inverse — the main unresolved gate

Establish a uniform estimate for all integers `n>=2` in the same norm used in A. Treat finite orders and the high-order tail separately if useful. The real-axis maximum-principle proof does not automatically apply at complex radial arguments. Either construct a justified complex extension of the inverse with a uniform bound or use another function space with a complete derivative-loss closure.

Do not infer a collar width from a few successful complex sample points. A numerical certificate must cover the whole proposed set, all admitted background parameters, endpoint maps and every order range.

**Gate B:** explicit common width and inverse constant with a proof covering finite orders plus the entire tail, or a documented unresolved obstruction. There is no obligation to make a radius look physically large.

### C. Neutral majorant and a posteriori remainder

Use the prescribed first-order seed as the linear term. There is no charge-forcing term to copy from the charged-decaying majorant. Bind the source constants from A and inverse constants from B. State a scalar majorant map, an invariant ball and a contraction/implicit-function criterion. Verify with directed or exact arithmetic.

Report separate certificates for existence/convergence and any sign claim about the actual nonlinear separation. A convergent series alone does not certify that a cubic truncation has the same sign as the full solution. Bound the tail of `D` and, if claimed, its proper-time derivative. At positive and negative amplitudes report the same fixed amplitude normalization.

**Gate C:** nonzero certified amplitude interval, tail bound and exact claim scope. If the radius is too small to resolve a nonlinear effect, record that honestly; do not reinterpret a formal coefficient as a finite-amplitude result.

## Useful outcomes

1. **Full bridge passes:** controlled local nonlinear growing development, with quantitative error. It still does not establish saturation, wall contact, collapse, topology change or a horizon.
2. **Convergence passes but effect below remainder:** existence is new; cubic-sign interpretation remains formal at the tested amplitude.
3. **Source budget fails:** identify the specific derivative/composition mismatch and redesign the analytic norm in a separate successor.
4. **Complex inverse not certified:** preserve the all-order real-axis result and the audited quadratic/cubic coefficients. Do not label the branch nonexistent.
