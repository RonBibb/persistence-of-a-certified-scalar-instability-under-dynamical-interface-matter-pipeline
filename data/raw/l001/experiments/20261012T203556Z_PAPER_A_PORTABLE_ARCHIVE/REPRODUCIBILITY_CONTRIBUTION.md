# Reproducibility of the static theorem evidence

The portable saved-evidence package replays two unchanged verification programs against byte-identical copies of their required records. Both programs passed on macOS under CPython 3.14.4, first in the staged archive and then in a relocated copy, with execution from an unrelated working directory. A separate auditor reproduced the four results by invoking the original programs directly, bypassing the packaging wrapper. These checks establish portability of the two saved-evidence verification layers on the tested host.

The publication-upgrade verifier checks the hashes of 17 bound records and six authority predicates. Its 23 successful checks confirm that the archived conclusions for independent D1 replay, first-event uniqueness, isolation, fixed-separation exclusion, and finite-resolution GH support agree with the frozen records. This layer reads previously produced conclusions; it does not reconstruct the global uniqueness proof or the isolation determinant calculation. The bundled GH records are contextual numerical support and are not a premise for the static theorem or a stability claim.

The consolidation verifier provides a distinct layer of reconstruction. It verifies 14 fixed hash anchors and six original manifests with their terminal bindings. Using exact rational endpoints, it checks that the saved 32768-step Newton image lies strictly inside the frozen narrow launch interval and the saved 16384-step image. It reconstructs the adjacency of the 693-box fixed-separation cover, all exclusion classifications, the empty unresolved set, and the residual-vector floor greater than 77/10000 on boxes not already excluded by curvature. It also verifies the canonical content addresses of all 86 saved checkpoints. Other predicates check the recorded precision, step count, derivative signs, and earlier audit conclusions.

These computations use stored enclosures as inputs. They do not recompute the ODE flow, Taylor or Picard remainder bounds, event or sensitivity enclosures, or the physical spectral equation. Historical producer source files included by the original manifests are hashed as data and are never imported or executed. The 693-box result retains its original assurance level: one full numerical producer and independent reconstruction of the stored cover, without a second full D2A integration.

All 21 source records selected for the seven paper claims are additionally available offline, along with the unchanged contribution and claim map. The later tension extension and central negative-mode result are documentary inclusions. Their presence does not imply that either result was replayed by this package. The source-to-archive map preserves the distinction between the central-mode producer's provisional record and its separate final acceptance, and it identifies the tension integrity record's lack of an independent neighborhood producer.

The remaining reproducibility gate is broader than these two successful programs. A full paper archive must include the recursive model-provenance and producer dependencies; the original failed runs required to interpret later accepted results; numerical library versions and arithmetic settings; and separately reviewed replay commands for the global first-event, isolation, tension, and spectral evidence. The independent D1 engine requires the `flint` arithmetic package. A clean Linux replay and numerical producer replication remain unperformed. The present package is complete for the two stated saved-evidence entrypoints and does not claim completeness for every proof dependency of the selected paper claims.

The scientific scope remains the specified reduced static family. Archive replay supplies no new conclusion about evolution, dynamical selection, collapse, MOTS, an event horizon, or a child universe. Manuscript integration and conventional citation review remain separate publication tasks.
