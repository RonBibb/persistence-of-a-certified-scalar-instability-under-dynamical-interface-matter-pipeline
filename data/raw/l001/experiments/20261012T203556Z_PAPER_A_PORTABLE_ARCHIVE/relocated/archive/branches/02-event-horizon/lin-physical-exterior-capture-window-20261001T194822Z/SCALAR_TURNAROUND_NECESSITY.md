# Scalar-turnaround necessity

## Statement

In the proper-distance static system

\[
 \Phi_s=S,\qquad S_s=m^2\Phi-4HS,
\]

assume \(m^2>0\), \(\gamma>0\), and \(\Phi>0\) between the walls. If an
admissible solution obeys

\[
 \Phi_a<v_0,\qquad \Phi_b<v_1,
\]

then its scalar profile has exactly one interior critical point, and that
point is a strict minimum.

## Proof

The lower half-Robin condition gives

\[
 S_a=\frac{\gamma}{2}(\Phi_a-v_0)<0.
\]

The upper half-Robin condition has the opposite inward-normal orientation:

\[
 S_b=-\frac{\gamma}{2}(\Phi_b-v_1)>0.
\]

Continuity therefore gives at least one point \(s_*\) with \(S(s_*)=0\).
At every such point the field equation reduces to

\[
 S_s(s_*)=m^2\Phi(s_*)>0.
\]

Every zero is consequently a simple crossing from negative to positive.
After one such crossing, a second zero would require a positive-to-negative
crossing somewhere in between, whose derivative could not be strictly
positive. Hence there is exactly one zero of \(S\), and because
\(\Phi_s=S\), it is a strict minimum of \(\Phi\).

## Application to the retained candidate

The retained branch has

\[
 \Phi_a\approx0.09901087<v_0=0.1,
\qquad
 \Phi_b\approx0.08832224<v_1\approx0.09048374.
\]

Its unique sampled turnaround at \(s\approx1.68919\) is therefore not an
accidental numerical feature; a turnaround is required by the two wall
orientations and the positive scalar mass term. The sampled location is still
finite-resolution data, but the existence and uniqueness of a turnaround are
analytic conditional statements for any exact positive solution with those
endpoint inequalities.

At the two first-Israel-crossing samples bracketing the collapse separation,
the scalar is still decreasing at the upper wall. They satisfy the upper
gravitational event but violate the required scalar Robin orientation by an
order-one residual. This identifies what the extra proper distance does on
the retained branch: it permits the required minimum and return toward the
upper target.

## Limit

The argument does not produce a lower bound on the separation and does not
exclude another branch that turns earlier. A rigorous fixed-separation
exclusion still requires a complete validated cover or a stronger analytic
inequality. It does establish that any successful alternative with the same
endpoint inequalities must contain the same qualitative scalar turnaround.
