# Finite wall stiffness and the prospective Paper A contribution

Increasing a brane potential's curvature can raise a scalar mode's mass. That effect, and the fact that fixing brane positions does not guarantee stability, are established literature. Paper A should not present either as a new mechanism. The prospective contribution is a constructive, validated finite continuation on its exact curved background using variables that remain regular at the interior scalar turn.

## Closest primary results

[Frolov and Kofman, Can Inflating Braneworlds be Stabilized?](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0309002v1) analyze five-dimensional Einstein–scalar models with de Sitter slices. Their finite wall boundary condition contains the potential Hessian; their analytic estimate uses a stiff-wall simplification. The shifted spectral parameter is physical mass squared plus four times the de Sitter curvature. Stiff walls alone do not ensure a positive physical mass. A new claim here therefore cannot be the discovery of a curvature-driven tachyon or the insufficiency of stiff boundary pinning. A regular finite-Hessian certificate on the present turning-scalar background would be a model-specific constructive complement, not a contradiction of that result. Relevant passages: Sections III–V, equations 14–20.

[Girmohanta et al., Note on Warped Compactification — Finite Brane Potentials and Non-Hermiticity](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05141v2) examine finite brane potentials in a Minkowski-sliced Goldberger–Wise setting, including perturbative and numerical spectra, tachyonic thresholds and the physical scalar product. Their finite-coupling mass response is directly relevant precedent. Different geometry and scalar profile mean their threshold cannot be imported into this model. A certified finite displacement here would extend the type of calculation to a different setting; it would not make stiffness-dependent radion masses novel. Relevant passages: introduction, Sections 2.2–4, equations 3.6–3.11.

[Olechowski, Stability of multibrane models](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15343v2) gives scalar stability conditions for flat-sliced multibrane systems. Section 4.3 explicitly varies wall-potential second derivatives while preserving the background, discusses monotonicity, and treats a vanishing bulk scalar derivative using a regular variable. This is particularly close precedent: neither background-preserving Hessian variation nor concern about a scalar turn is new. The present route could add a quantitative curved-background branch certificate and bounds under the declared action. The full spectral conclusions of that work cannot simply be assumed for the present constrained curved system. Relevant passages: Sections 4.2–4.3, equations 48–55 and footnote 6.

## Prospective claim and novelty uncertainty

An earlier close antecedent is [Lesgourgues and Sorbo, Goldberger–Wise variations](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0310007). It treats Minkowski-sliced backgrounds with arbitrary bulk and wall scalar potentials, develops regular variables across a scalar turn, and derives tachyon criteria through continuous shooting determinants. A sign-bracket argument for instability is therefore also an established method. The present added value must lie in the curved, exact-background certificate and its finite quantitative continuation, not in claiming to invent the shooting argument.

The independently accepted results now supply that finite branch: a sixfold upper-wall Hessian change preserves the exact background and gives a strictly positive certified mode displacement. The regular-variable proof, with its validated endpoint premises, extends the tracked branch to every finite nonnegative upper-wall increment and identifies a still-negative Dirichlet limit. See `TERMINAL_REVIEW.json` and `FINITE_BRANCH_PROOF.md`. The quantitative upper-only limiting eigenvalue is accepted in `DIRICHLET_TERMINAL_REVIEW.json`. The separate analytic review `TWO_WALL_REVIEW.json` accepts a smooth response surface for both nonnegative wall increments, including a qualitative double-Dirichlet maximum. These are model-specific constructive results, not assertions of novel generic stiffness dependence. The one-wall numerical limit must not be substituted for the unquantified two-wall maximum.

The potential distinction is the combination of an exact-background existence certificate, regular treatment across the scalar turn, positive physical norm, and validated finite spectral continuation. This focused comparison does not establish priority or exhaust prior literature. Full-spectrum and nonlinear stability remain separate questions. The primary texts support the comparisons above; they do not validate the new calculation.
