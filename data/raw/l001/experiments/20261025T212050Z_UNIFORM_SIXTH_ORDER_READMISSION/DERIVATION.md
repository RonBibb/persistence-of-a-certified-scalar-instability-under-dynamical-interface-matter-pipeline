# Factored characteristic and continuous parameter certificate

Let C(d,beta)=F(1) for the scaled system with lower boundary F(-1)=0, psi(-1)=1 and G(-1)=3alpha/[a(-1)^2 tau(-1)]. Set alpha=-2/3+20d^2/9+beta d^4 and lambda=d^2 alpha. Every background and launch quantity depends on d; none is frozen at a sampled width.

The exact radial-series control solves the lower-wall equation alone for the central slope B(d). Odd coefficients of B are allowed. Through total degree six, the normalized upper residual has zero coefficients in d^0,d^1,d^2,d^3 and coefficient -6(beta+383/15) in d^4. The lower normalization is nonsingular near zero since its constant term is one. The scaled ODE and launch are smooth on the admitted background domain, including the desingularized limit at d=0 where tau(-1)=-1.

Taylor's integral remainder therefore gives

C(d,beta)/d^4 = 4 integral_0^1 (1-t)^3 [partial_d^4 C(td,beta)/4!] dt.

The weight integrates to one and is nonnegative. Bounding the fourth derivative coefficient throughout [0,.01] therefore bounds the factored characteristic without dividing by d. The production state stores the fourth coefficient for the seed d=q+s epsilon, s=1/100. Its value is s^4 partial_d^4 C(q,beta)/4!, so division by the fixed positive rational s^4 is safe. This is not division by an interval width.

At beta=-766/25 the limiting coefficient is +766/25. At beta=-1532/75 it is -766/25. If the continuous cover certifies the respective signs everywhere, the normalized characteristic has opposite signs at the two beta endpoints for every d>0. Continuity gives a root between them.

The parameterized alpha endpoints stay inside the previously certified interval [-4/5,-8/15]. The single-turn and endpoint hypotheses are checked afresh during propagation; the accepted DD root-count argument identifies the same persistent branch. Reversing lambda's sign and writing D=2d maps the beta bracket exactly to the requested sixth-order coefficient envelope [383/1200,383/800].

The zero-width endpoint is a smooth limiting calculation, not a zero-size physical eigenmode. No certified counterexample follows from enclosure failure alone.
