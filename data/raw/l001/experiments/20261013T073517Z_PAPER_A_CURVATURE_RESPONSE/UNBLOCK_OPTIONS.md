## What the literature resolves

Scalar-supported selection of separation and curved branes are established ingredients. DeWolfe, Freedman, Gubser and Karch explicitly discuss nonzero four-dimensional curvature and generic local selection in section 3.2. Their curvature parameter and warp normalization are linked; a curvature comparison needs a specified physical wall or normalization. Our lower-wall scalar R=12k with A(0)=0 supplies that specification. The contribution here is a certified response in one fixed finite-stiffness model, not discovery of the general tension-curvature connection. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/9909134v4#S3.SS2)

Static response is not time evolution. Martin and collaborators evolve examples with curved branes, instability and transitions between equilibria. Their figures and sections 5.1–5.2 concern particular dynamical models, not a theorem that our tension-parameter curve is followed in time. They motivate asking whether a certified equilibrium is unstable, but cannot choose the nonlinear endpoint of our branch. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0309001v1)

Frolov and Kofman's scalar analysis first retains finite wall potentials, then simplifies to stiff Dirichlet scalar conditions in equations 14–17. Their weighted formulation divides by the background scalar gradient; its Rayleigh argument uses the resulting operator and boundary domain. Our finite stiffness and scalar-gradient zero therefore require an applicability derivation, not direct substitution into the stiff formula. The saved negative value of mu+4k is not, by itself, a contradiction of that approximation. [Primary full text, sections III–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0309002v1)

Two later comparisons reinforce this distinction. Lesgourgues and Sorbo explicitly start with Minkowski sections. Girmohanta and collaborators also study Minkowski branes, and show that finite wall potentials can alter scalar-mode boundary products and instability. These provide methods and questions, not transferable answers for a curved background with a different action. In particular, a boundary-corrected product in transformed variables is not evidence that our regular coupled-variable norm has omitted a physical action term. [Lesgourgues and Sorbo](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0310007v2), [Girmohanta and collaborators, section 2.2 and appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05141v2)

For stronger independent numerical proofs, domain-decomposed Chebyshev expansions and radii-polynomial contraction bounds offer an alternative to interval shooting. Van den Berg and Sheombarsing demonstrate this method for analytic ODE boundary-value problems. Adapting it here would require the same action, boundary conditions, event definition and error bounds; a spectral approximation alone would not constitute validation or global exclusion. [Primary publication](https://www.aimsciences.org/article/doi/10.3934/jcd.2021015)
