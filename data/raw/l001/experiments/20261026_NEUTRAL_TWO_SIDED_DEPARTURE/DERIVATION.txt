Two-sided local behavior and a scalar-observable departure witness

This uses the same certified analytic pure-growing family, not new ODE data.
All conclusions are confined to the conditioned proper-clock subdisk.
The initial-data statement below is a constructed family of compatible
data and solutions, not a general Cauchy well-posedness theorem.

1. The two signs of the growing amplitude
Let a=|eta|<=eta_star, the conditioned clock amplitude. The signed
quadratic coefficient of F=dx/dtau_a-rx obeys -50<F2<-21 when both
engine enclosures certify -54<d1<-50 and -1600<d2<-1400, with
1/64<r<=1/32. Indeed -1550<d2-d1<-1346. The absolute remainder bound
is sign-independent and <=10a². Consequently
 -60a²<=F(eta)<=-11a².
The distance remainder after linear order is <=25a, giving
 25a<=|x|<=79a, sign(x)=-sign(eta).
For eta>0, dx/dtau_a<rx<0: shortening is reinforced.
For eta<0, dx/dtau_a>=25a/64-60a²>0 if a<1/512, while dx/dtau_a<rx:
opening is slowed relative to the linear law, not reversed or stabilized.
These are local statements in the specified foliation and lower-wall clock.

2. An independent physical observable: the lower wall oscillator
The neutral background oscillator is exactly zero. Its normalized linear
coefficient is chi1_a=f1_a/(2-mu1), because E_a=1 and xi1_a=0.
Authenticate both fresh initial-state enclosures and require
 1/20000<chi1_a<1/10000.
The aggregate coefficient majorant bounds |chi_a|<=R on the physical
amplitude disk rho_D, since the oscillator's n³ coefficient weight is
part of Y_n and n>=1. Let q=a/rho_D. Cauchy gives
 |chi_a-chi1_a eta|<=R q²/(1-q),
 |eta chi_a'-chi1_a eta|<=R q²(2-q)/(1-q)².
Require both bounds <=a/40000. These inequalities then hold for every
smaller positive a by monotonicity of the corresponding ratios to a.
They imply sign(chi_a)=sign(eta) and |chi_a|>=a/40000. Also
sign(dchi_a/dtau_a)=sign(eta), since r>0 and the real lapse is positive.
The oscillator therefore departs monotonically from its zero background.
Its nonzero value cannot be obtained by a coordinate transformation of
an identically zero scalar field. This avoids relying only on distance
in a chosen slicing to establish nontrivial matter evolution.

3. A precise, limited initial-data consequence
Fix eta_star>0 admitted by the preceding test, and consider any
epsilon in(0,eta_star). The exact solution eta(t)=epsilon exp(rt)
reaches eta_star at coordinate time T=(1/r)log(eta_star/epsilon).
Its lower-wall oscillator then has chi_a>=eta_star/40000=:delta_star>0.
At t=0 the pulled metric/scalar perturbations and their fixed radial/time
derivatives, both wall displacements and velocities, and both oscillator
data tend to zero as epsilon tends to zero. This follows from the proven
analytic series with zero constant perturbation coefficient and uniform
fixed-derivative convergence on the real interval. Restrictions of the
exact solution satisfy the bulk and wall initial compatibility conditions.

One concrete data topology is C² on the pulled bulk fields, C¹ on their
first time derivatives, and Euclidean norm on wall positions/velocities
and oscillator positions/velocities, always relative to the same background
and with the lower-wall clock normalization. The constructed data tend to
zero in this topology. The departure criterion is the physical scalar
measurement |chi_a|>=delta_star, not a coordinate norm alone.

The positive real lapse is bounded below by1/128 from the prior inverse
lapse bound. It is bounded above by8 because E>=1/2-delta>1/4,
exp(p)<=1/(1-R)<2 and sqrt(1-v²)<=1. Thus T/128<=Delta tau_a<=8T,
so each such departure occurs at finite lower-wall proper time.

This supplies an arbitrarily-small-compatible-data departure witness
along this particular analytic family. It does not supply existence,
uniqueness or continuous dependence for a neighborhood of general data,
a full unstable-manifold theorem, or an endpoint beyond eta_star.
The tiny positive delta_star is a proof threshold, not a physical prediction.
