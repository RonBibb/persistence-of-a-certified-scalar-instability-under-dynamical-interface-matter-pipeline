# Lowest regular mode and ordered growth rates

Status: proposed consequence for independent review. This relies on the accepted exact turning index, spectral reality and de Sitter growth formula. It makes no operator-completeness claim.

All regular admissible scalar eigenvalues are real. If the exact scalar-turn count is n=1, there is exactly one such eigenvalue μ_1<-4k. Every other regular admissible scalar eigenvalue must lie above -4k: another value below would violate the index, and the auxiliary determinant excludes equality. Therefore μ_1 is the strictly lowest regular admissible scalar eigenvalue of this declared boundary problem.

For a negative physical mass μ, the growing de Sitter amplitude exponent is

    σ(μ)=(√(9k-4μ)-3√k)/2,
    dσ/dμ=-1/√(9k-4μ)<0.

The unique μ_1<-4k thus has σ_1>H and is faster than every other regular scalar tachyonic separated mode, should any lie in -4k<μ<0. Those additional modes would have 0<σ<H. No regular mode sits exactly at the intermediate growth rate H because μ=-4k is excluded. Nonnegative masses do not supply a positive late-time tachyonic amplitude exponent.

More generally, for n turns the n ordered negative-shifted eigenvalues are precisely the regular radial scalar channels whose tachyonic de Sitter amplitude exponent exceeds H. Their mass order reverses their growth-rate order. The positive regular norm does not convert their negative masses into stable oscillations.

This applies to the inherited central single-turn background, and to the convex-range class, at every wall pair admitted by their respective reviewed theorems. It is now justified to identify the central unique branch as the lowest regular scalar mass; the earlier predecessor's inability to identify a selected mode as lowest has been resolved for this application. For a general multi-turn selected branch, lowest-mode identity still depends on its ordered label.

The result does not assert completeness of the radial modes, stability of other perturbation sectors, or dominance for arbitrary initial data without a mode expansion. It orders the admissible separated scalar modes and their constructed finite-packet amplitude growth, and supplies no nonlinear fate.
