# Scope of the general continuation claim

The strongest contribution candidate is a regular constrained index and continuation theorem with checkable background hypotheses, rather than the generic observation that boundary stiffness moves eigenvalues. Independent analytic reviews in this packet accept seed-free existence, an exact count below the curvature-shifted threshold, and controlled two-wall stiff-limit laws. A convex-range extension supplies exactly one such mode for every nonnegative pair of wall Hessians, including zero-Hessian walls and Dirichlet limits. Mathematical review acceptance does not establish historical priority or publication novelty.

## Curved braneworld perturbations

[Frolov and Kofman, Can Inflating Braneworlds be Stabilized?](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0309002v1), Sections II–IV, already treat de Sitter slices with arbitrary bulk scalar potentials and finite wall-potential Hessians. Equations 8–15 exhibit the curvature shift, scalar perturbation system and Hessian-dependent boundary conditions. Their reduced variables contain divisions by the background scalar derivative. Thus arbitrary potential or curved slices alone cannot establish novelty here. The relevant comparison is an explicit regular proof through a scalar turn, with finite boundary control and a certified example. This is a difference in proof scope to investigate, not an assertion that the earlier physics is wrong.

## Scalar turns and background preserving wall changes

[Lesgourgues and Sorbo, Goldberger–Wise variations](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0310007), Sections II–III, develop regular variables and sign-based shooting arguments for flat-sliced scalar branes. Their two-brane stability conditions include assumptions on the warp factor and scalar derivative. Existence of a tachyon associated with scalar turning is established precedent. A seed-free shooting argument must therefore be presented as a curved, constrained-system theorem under its stated hypotheses, not as a newly discovered general instability mechanism.

[Olechowski, Stability of multibrane models](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15343v2), Sections 4.2–4.3, gives flat-sliced multibrane stability criteria. Footnote 6 explicitly explains changing wall Hessians while preserving the background; the following argument establishes monotonicity of the lowest eigenvalue. Equations 51–55 use a regular variable at scalar-derivative zeros. Our tracked-mode theorem must not be advertised as stronger full-spectrum information. A potential distinction is a regular de Sitter proof that does not require dividing by either the scalar derivative or warp derivative, together with explicit continuation and saturation data. Priority remains unresolved.

## Finite coupling and stiff limit asymptotics

[Girmohanta et al., Note on Warped Compactification—Finite Brane Potentials and Non-Hermiticity](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05141v2) studies finite brane potentials, scalar products and scalar spectra in a warped compactification setting. Finite-coupling eigenvalue shifts are therefore existing physics. Any proposed extension must retain the action-derived norm and boundary conditions rather than borrow an unrelated spectral inner product.

[Ognibene, On asymptotics of Robin eigenvalues in the Dirichlet limit](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.19505), introduction and Theorem 1.1, treats Robin Laplacian limits and first-order boundary-controlled eigenvalue corrections, including multiple Dirichlet eigenvalues. Its introduction records older one-dimensional expansions. Consequently an inverse-stiffness expansion is not generically new. A useful result here would supply the appropriate coefficients and remainder for the constrained Einstein–scalar system, including its scalar turn and two independent walls. A derived allocation rule would be a mathematical control corollary, not a dynamical law requiring nature to optimize wall stiffness.

## Paper positioning

[Chen, Gu and Liu, Stability of braneworlds with non-minimally coupled multi-scalar fields](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjc/s10052-018-5613-7), Section 3, already applies nodal counting to coupled scalar perturbation systems on flat thick-brane backgrounds. It distinguishes the boundary sectors where the theorem applies from others needing additional justification. This is direct precedent for using zero-mode structure to count unstable modes in braneworld physics. Our exact turning index is therefore not a new nodal principle: its possible contribution is the explicit reduction to background scalar turns for this curved finite-wall constrained problem, with the corner phase lift and wall conditions proved rather than borrowed. No claim that the cited thick-brane domain is equivalent to ours is made.

[Konikowska, Olechowski and Schmidt, Radion stabilization with(out) Gauss–Bonnet interactions and inflation](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0603014v2) is a particularly close antecedent. Its Einstein limit includes curved slices, finite wall Hessians and the shifted eigenvalue in equations 20–29. Section 4 restricts its subsequent analytic stability proof to zero Hubble parameter after equation 30; footnote 3 explains why the simple transformed equations do not extend directly to nonzero curvature. Equations 44–46 and their discussion already connect scalar turns with instability and use large-negative-mass shooting. Thus neither finite Hessians nor scalar-turn instability is new. The present proposed distinction is the regular curved-sector proof, explicit uniform barriers and exact restricted turning-count theorem, subject to its own review—not a replacement of that paper's broader action or static stability results.

[Contaldi, Kofman and Peloso, Gravitational Instability of de Sitter Compactifications](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0403270v1), Sections III–IV, derives the curvature contribution to scalar masses and discusses five-dimensional scalar stabilization. Equations 17–20 give tachyonic time growth; equations 21–26 specialize to braneworlds. The curvature shift and rapid growth are therefore established physics. Our strict bound below the shifted threshold and any count theorem must be presented as consequences of the supplied canonical-background hypotheses. The present packet establishes neither their general higher-dimensional conjecture nor their model-specific nonlinear outcomes.

[Carena et al., Self-accelerating Warped Braneworlds](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0611157), Section III, uses endpoint signs of spectral determinants to infer odd or even mode counts. Its induced-gravity action and mode sectors differ from the present pure-potential Einstein–scalar problem. Determinant sign parity itself is established mathematics and already appears in braneworld physics; the new restricted-sector count must be tied to its specific regular determinant, uniform barriers and parameter domain.

[Martin et al., Braneworld Dynamics with the BraneCode](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0309001), Section 5.1, equations 30–32, gives the de Sitter tachyon growth exponent and compares it with numerical evolution. Section 5.2 exhibits different nonlinear outcomes for particular models. Our growth-rate consequence cannot be claimed as a new time-evolution formula, and those nonlinear outcomes cannot be imported into the present background. Its potential added content is the rigorous spectral inequality yielding a strict rate bound for the stated class.

The count, growth and response estimates have separate receipts. The central application enlarges narrow numerical-strip uniqueness to the entire negative-shifted sector; it does not count additional physical tachyons above that threshold. This targeted comparison does not establish publication priority or exhaust the literature. The defensible emphasis is the reusable regular proof, its checkable hypotheses and its connection to the certified example—not a claim to have discovered tachyons, nodal counting, monotonic Robin spectra, determinant parity or de Sitter growth.
