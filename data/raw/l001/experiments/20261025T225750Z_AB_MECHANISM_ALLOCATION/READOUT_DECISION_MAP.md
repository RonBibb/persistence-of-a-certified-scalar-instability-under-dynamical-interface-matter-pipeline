# What the additional clock measurement can decide

Prospective interpretation map for T13, written before its result. This is not a numerical result, a new execution authorization, or a revision of T12.

## Question

The accepted calculations describe three infinitesimal response directions on the selected AB background: one neutral growing mode and two independently sourced, decaying first-current responses. Their individual distance and clock signatures differ. T12 nevertheless leaves joint linear distinguishability unresolved at the retained enclosures.

An observer at the other interface measures a different elapsed proper time along a different pair of null paths. Adding this fourth readout tests whether that observable separates combinations left unresolved by the original three. It does not add another source or change the field equations.

## Outcomes fixed before measurement

- At least one of the four predeclared three-by-three minors has an authoritative enclosure excluding zero: the four-readout map has full column rank for the three declared formal directions. Report every minor, including unresolved ones. This establishes local linear distinguishability, not reliable inversion in noisy data or a nonlinear reconstruction.
- Every minor enclosure contains zero: the augmented rank test remains unresolved. Do not infer an exact physical degeneracy or select a midpoint determinant. Record which readouts and uncertainty dependencies limit the proof before proposing a successor.
- A source binding, clock derivation, normalization, interval-domain or resource gate fails: technical incompleteness. It supplies no physical rank conclusion.

The current-square coefficients have a nonnegative physical domain. Full rank in the larger formal signed response space would also distinguish points within that cone; failure to certify full rank does not demonstrate non-identifiability within the cone. All amplitudes are defined at the common emission event, despite their different time laws.

## What remains a different question

Distinguishing a neutral mode from a current-driven response does not show that turning on current excites that mode. Excitation requires specified initial data or a source history and the appropriate action-derived evolution and boundary compatibility. A static response ratio cannot replace those inputs.

Likewise T11 supplies a required quadratic chart-metric combination, not the complete second-order distance or clock response. Obtaining the latter requires the remaining second-order fields, moving-boundary conditions and observable reconstruction. A coefficient alone does not provide a finite-amplitude remainder bound.

The next research choice after T13 should therefore distinguish measurement design from source-to-mode dynamics. More precise readouts help identify a response; an initial-value calculation answers how it is produced. These are complementary tasks, not interchangeable evidence.
