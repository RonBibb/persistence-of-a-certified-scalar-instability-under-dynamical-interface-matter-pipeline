This is a derivation for review, not an admitted calculation. It changes no sealed predecessor and does not authorize execution by itself.

## Question

Does the first current-driven separation correction represent shortening on both sides of the bulk scalar minimum, or shortening on one side accompanied by lengthening on the other? A physical internal marker can distinguish those possibilities without interpreting coordinate-dependent bulk and graph pieces as separate observables.

Use the actual AB background and the same specified redshift cuts as T4. Let s_star denote its unique simple scalar minimum, with T0(s_star)=0 and T0_prime(s_star)>0. For each first-current channel, the scalar is Phi0(s)+z f1(s), the radial metric factor is exp(b)=1−2z p1(s)+O(z²), and interface locations are s_i+z xi_i. The minimum location on those cuts is s_star+z xi_star+O(z²), where

`xi_star = −g1(s_star)/T0_prime(s_star)`.

The coordinate displacement xi_star is not itself an invariant observable. The scalar value change at the minimum is `f1(s_star)`, since its background radial derivative vanishes.

## Proper-distance responses

`C_left = −2 integral[a,s_star](p1 ds) + xi_star − xi_a`,

`C_right = −2 integral[s_star,b](p1 ds) + xi_b − xi_star`.

Their exact sum is T4's total coefficient. Each length includes its moving endpoints; neither is just an integral of the metric perturbation. Under a transverse coordinate relabeling, the first variation of the metric integral cancels the endpoint changes. This does not establish equivalence to a distinct finite-charge clock prescription. The observable remains a proper length on the declared cuts.

Separate current labels and normalization by neutral local proper density must both be retained. A negative coefficient is a shorter first-order offset; because z decays, it is not an assertion of continuing contraction. The neutral unstable mode is a different history with a freely specified amplitude.
